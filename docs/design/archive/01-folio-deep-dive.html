印刻·Folio

Personal-Studio Design Proposal · No. 01
2026 · 4 · 21

A student's tool shouldn't look like a chat app. It should feel like a thoughtful notebook — one you're proud to open at 2 a.m.

Thesis

Reject the genre's defaults. Most "AI for students" apps converge on the same glassy dark-mode-plus-purple-gradient aesthetic borrowed from ChatGPT clones. We go the other way.

印刻 · Folio is a scholarly publishing language applied to a live, interactive app: warm paper, deep ink, one vermilion seal for meaning, crafted serifs for every sentence that matters. Every screen reads like a page from a well-kept study journal. Every formula sits in properly set italic display type, not sans-serif.

The design is intentionally not trendy. It will still feel fresh in three years.

朱砂 (Vermilion) is the punctuation — used like a scholar's seal or a red-pen annotation, never decoratively. Cerulean is for formulas and code (fountain-pen ink). Olive marks progress; amber marks time pressure. The dark theme inverts: deep navy paper, warm bone ink.

The display face is Fraunces — a variable serif with an SOFT axis that gets rounder at larger sizes, and an opsz axis that tightens at small sizes. It has genuine warmth, unlike the polished precision of Bodoni/Playfair. The italic accent is Instrument Serif, whose italic is a real italic (different letterforms, not just slanted). The body Instrument Sans is a humanist sans with a quiet voice. Chinese uses Noto Serif SC / Sans SC as fallbacks — both are Adobe-partner grade and sit naturally alongside the Latin faces.

§ 04

Guiding Principles

decisions already made
01 · TYPE

Serif carries weight; sans carries work.

Titles, conclusions, and formulas are set in Fraunces. Instrument Serif italic is the voice of the system — labels, transitions, gentle UI prompts. Body copy and lists are Instrument Sans. Every byte of JetBrains Mono is intentional — metadata, code, timestamps.
02 · COLOR

Ink and paper, with one red pen.

The base is warm paper plus deep blue-black ink (not pure black — ink on paper is never #000). Vermilion is reserved for signals with meaning: new items, deadlines, "now," AI-generated stamps. Never decoratively.
03 · RULE & GRID

Horizontal rules over boxes.

Content is separated by thin rules and dashed lines instead of drop-shadow cards. Lists look like ledgers. The "phone app" visual language dissolves into something closer to a typeset page.
04 · SURFACE

Always a little grain.

Every surface has a faint paper-noise overlay (~5% opacity). Buttons and frames use offset shadow (like block-printed drop shadow) rather than Material 3's soft elevation. The tactile quality is the brand.
05 · MOTION

As if written, not animated.

Streaming AI text pens onto the page character-by-character with a soft fade — not a bouncy typing indicator. Page transitions are crossfades over paper, not slides. Everything is ease-out 240-360ms. No spring animations.
06 · LANGUAGE

Name things like a scholar would.

"付梓" (send to press) instead of "Send". "存入手札" (enter into folio) instead of "Save". "取景" (frame) for Scanner capture. Every micro-copy carries the world. Keep English labels where they clarify (JSON, API, PDF).
§ 05

Paths not taken

two alternate aesthetics, briefly
Option B

Graphite Atlas

Technical-manual / cartographic. Monochrome graphite + signal orange. Thin line work, tick marks, dense legend-style metadata. Smart but colder — more "power tool" than "study companion."

Option A · proposed

印刻 · Folio — above

Scholarly publishing applied to a live app. Warm, crafted, Chinese-academic. Hardest to confuse with any competitor. The one I'd ship.

Option C

Midnight Codex

Dark only, deep teal base + persimmon accent. "Late-night study lamp" mood. Warm, but resolutely dark — would constrain daytime use.
